Choose the region first, click last

  • 🤔Point-first methods lose the target. Existing training-free methods zoom around a predicted click, so a click that lands off the target leaves it outside every later view.
  • 🔍Region-first cropping. The VLM chooses the region and scale of each next view itself, and clicks only once, on the final view.
  • 📍Coordinate references and verification. OCR text and detected UI components are listed with their positions, and every crop and click is checked against the instruction.
  • ✅Training-free. GUI-Lens works with any API VLM and raises ScreenSpot-Pro accuracy by up to 9.3× over the direct click of the same base model.

Figure 1. Top: on a UI-Vision example, point-first zooming loses the target, while region-first cropping (GUI-Lens) keeps it. Bottom: ScreenSpot-Pro accuracy (%) of six base models with direct clicking and with GUI-Lens.

Abstract

GUI grounding maps natural-language instructions to click locations and is essential for reliable GUI agents. The task remains difficult on high-resolution, densely populated interfaces because a vision-language model (VLM) may recognize a requested control without locating it precisely enough for interaction. Existing training-free methods are point-first: they zoom around a predicted click, so every view depends on a coordinate committed at a coarser scale.

In this paper, we introduce GUI-Lens, a region-first grounding framework in which a general-purpose VLM selects each next view itself and clicks only once, on the final view. Specifically, GUI-Lens extracts OCR text and detected UI components from the screenshot and presents their positions as coordinate references. Using the instruction, the current view, and these references, the VLM selects the region and scale of the next view, which is cropped and enlarged to provide finer visual details. This process continues over successively focused views until the target is determined. Proposed crops and clicks are checked against the instruction throughout the process, and the final local position is mapped back to the original screen coordinates.

Across eight base models, the gain from cropping grows with reasoning, near-miss direct clicks, and low input resolution. Experiments on five GUI grounding benchmarks and six general-purpose base models show that GUI-Lens consistently improves grounding accuracy, by up to 9.3× on ScreenSpot-Pro.

Which Models Benefit from Cropping

The gain of coarse-to-fine cropping over the direct click varies widely across base models. On eight base models whose direct-click accuracy ranges from 1% to 96%, a few single-call probes predict this gain within 3.3 points for a base model left out of the fit, at 500 calls per base model.

Figure 2. Probes and gain of coarse-to-fine cropping on eight base models, one row each. Left: probe inputs, colored by the term they enter. Middle: expected gain by term, with the base model held out of the fit. Right: direct click, GUI-Lens, and expected accuracy.

  • 🧠Reasoning matters most. Without the reasoning term, the held-out error rises from 3.3 to 12.5 points. A base model that reasons checks each crop and discards the wrong ones.
  • 🎯Zooming works near the target. A crop enlarges the area around the model's own guess, so it fixes clicks that land close to the target.
  • 🖼️Cropping restores lost resolution. An endpoint that downscales the screenshot discards detail, and a crop brings it back.

Figure 3. Accuracy of direct clicking and GUI-Lens by target size in vision patches, over the eight base models. Direct clicks succeed on 7% of targets below half a patch, against about 60% for targets of one patch or more. GUI-Lens recovers about 30 points at every size.

Method

Figure 4. Overview of GUI-Lens. Coordinate Priming converts OCR text and detected UI components into coordinate references. Using the task, the current crop and the visible references, the VLM either selects a finer crop or proposes a click. Accepted crops advance the process, rejected proposals restart it, and the accepted final click is mapped back to the original screen.

Where the Gain Comes From

We group every example by where the direct click of the base model lands: Hit (inside the target), Near (the quarter-screen window around the click contains the target), and Far (otherwise).

GUI-Lens recovers targets the direct click misses. Recovered Near examples add 14.6 to 26.9 points of accuracy, and recovered Far examples add a further 1.5 to 7.8 points.

Figure 6. Where the gain of GUI-Lens over the direct click comes from, on UI-Vision and MMBench-GUI-L2. The direct click is correct on Hit and wrong on Near and Far examples; GUI-Lens stays correct on Hit examples and recovers both Near and Far ones.
